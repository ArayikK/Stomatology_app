STOM - COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE & FEATURE IDEAS
=============================================


1) SIMILAR APPS / DENTAL CHARTING SOFTWARE
-------------------------------------------
These are the established players in dental practice management and
odontogram (tooth-chart) software - useful references for UX and feature
ideas:

- Curve Dental (Curve Hero / Curve Core)
  Cloud-based practice management suite with a strong odontogram/charting
  module, imaging, scheduling and billing all in one place. Probably the
  closest match to what "Stom" is becoming.

- Dentrix (Henry Schein)
  One of the most widely used desktop practice management systems in the
  US. Deep charting, treatment planning, and insurance/billing tools.

- Eaglesoft (Patterson Dental)
  Similar market position to Dentrix - charting, imaging, scheduling,
  billing, all tightly integrated.

- Open Dental
  Open-source practice management software. Good reference because the
  source is publicly readable - useful if you want to see how they model
  tooth surfaces, procedures, and perio charting in a real schema.

- Denticon (Planet DDS)
  Cloud-based, popular with multi-location dental service organizations
  (DSOs); strong reporting/analytics on top of charting.

- Carestream Dental (CS SoftDent / CS OrthoTrac)
  Strong on the imaging side (X-ray/DICOM integration) paired with
  charting and practice management.

- iDentalSoft / MOGO / tab32 / SolutionReach / Weave
  Smaller / newer cloud competitors, several lean heavily on patient
  communication (reminders, texting, online booking) as a differentiator
  rather than charting depth.

- Google/Apple Health-adjacent consumer apps (e.g. "Toothie", various
  home tooth-tracking apps)
  Not real practice-management tools, but worth a glance for simple,
  friendly odontogram visuals aimed at patients rather than clinicians.

Positioning note: most of the big names above are full practice-management
suites (scheduling + billing + insurance + charting). Stom is currently a
focused charting/notes/x-ray tool, which is closer to a lightweight,
single-clinician alternative - that focus can be a strength if the goal is
"do charting really well" rather than "replace Dentrix."


2) FEATURE IDEAS FOR STOM
--------------------------

Charting depth
- Tooth-surface-level charting (mesial / distal / occlusal / buccal /
  lingual) instead of one note bucket per whole tooth - this is the
  standard clinical model and would make notes much more precise.
- A proper status/legend per tooth beyond "has history": missing, crown,
  filling, root canal, implant, extraction planned, watch. Each with its
  own color, shown as a small legend under the chart.
- Perio charting (probing depths, gum recession, bleeding on probing) as
  a second view alongside the odontogram - standard in every competitor
  listed above.
- Quick-pick procedure list (common treatments, ideally mapped to CDT
  procedure codes) instead of always typing free-text notes, with the
  option to still add a free-text note on top.

Patient record depth
- Treatment history timeline per patient (chronological feed across all
  teeth, not just per-tooth).
- Medical history / allergies / medications section per patient - important
  before doing any real clinical work.
- Search and filter on the patient list (by name, by "has open issues",
  by last visit date).
- Family/relationship linking between patient records.

Imaging
- Support DICOM (the real radiograph format), not just JPEG/PNG - or at
  minimum, image annotation/measurement tools on top of the current
  upload/view flow.
- Before/after image pairs per tooth.

Scheduling & communication
- Appointment calendar with reminders (this is the single biggest feature
  gap vs. every competitor above).
- SMS/email reminders and recall notifications ("due for checkup").

Data safety & access
- Cloud sync/backup - right now data lives only in a local SQLite file,
  so losing the device loses all patient records. This is the highest-risk
  gap for a real clinical tool.
- Multi-user accounts with roles (dentist / hygienist / front desk) and
  basic access control.
- Encryption at rest and an audit log (who viewed/edited what, when) -
  expected for handling real patient health data.
- Export a patient's chart/notes to PDF (for referrals, insurance, or
  patient handouts).

Day-to-day usability
- Voice-to-text note dictation for hands-free charting chairside.
- Undo/redo and note edit history (see what a note used to say).
- Tablet/stylus-optimized layout for chairside use during procedures.
- Dark mode.

Business side (bigger scope, only if Stom grows toward full practice
management)
- Billing/invoicing and insurance claim tracking.
- Basic reporting (procedures done per month, revenue per patient, etc.)

Suggested near-term priorities (highest impact, lowest effort first):
1. Cloud sync/backup - protects against data loss, the most serious risk
   right now.
2. Tooth-surface-level charting - meaningfully upgrades clinical accuracy
   without a huge UI overhaul.
3. Quick-pick procedure list w/ status legend - makes charting faster and
   the highlighted teeth more informative than a single blue on/off state.
4. Patient search/filter + medical history section - small effort, real
   day-to-day value.
5. Appointment scheduling - biggest feature gap vs. competitors, but also
   the biggest scope increase, so worth sequencing after the above.

3) THINGS YOU MUST DO, STEP BY STEP
-------------------------------------
Everything below is manual - things only you can do (accounts, credentials,
deployment). The code and docs referenced already exist in the project.

1. Create your MongoDB Atlas cluster.
   Full click-by-click steps are in backend/README.md, section 1. Short
   version: log into cloud.mongodb.com -> "Build a Database" -> M0 Free
   tier -> create a database user (username + password) -> Network Access
   -> "Add My Current IP Address" for now -> copy the connection string.

2. Run the backend locally and test it.
   backend/README.md, section 2:
     cd backend
     npm install
     copy .env.example .env      (then paste your real connection string
                                   into MONGODB_URI inside .env)
     npm start
   Confirm you see "Connected to MongoDB" and "Stom backend listening on
   port 3000", then test with the curl commands in that section.

3. Deploy the backend so it has a real public URL.
   backend/README.md, section 3: push the project to GitHub, create a
   free Web Service on render.com pointed at the backend folder, add the
   MONGODB_URI environment variable there too, and in Atlas -> Network
   Access add 0.0.0.0/0 (allow from anywhere) since Render's free tier
   doesn't use a fixed IP. Render will give you a URL like
   https://stom-backend.onrender.com.

4. Point the app at your real backend URL.
   Open lib/data/backend_sync_service.dart and replace the placeholder
   value of kBackendBaseUrl with the real Render URL from step 3. (Or send
   me the URL and I'll do it.)

5. Rebuild the APK and test sync end to end.
   flutter build apk --debug (or --release when ready), install it,
   add/edit a patient or note, then use the overflow menu's "Sync now" to
   confirm it succeeds, and "Device identifier" to see the id being used.

6. Get the Privacy Policy and Terms & Conditions reviewed by a lawyer.
   The text in lib/legal/legal_content.dart is complete and specific to
   how the app works, but it handles patient health information, so it
   should be checked against whatever laws apply to you and your patients
   (e.g. HIPAA, GDPR, or local equivalents) before you rely on it for real
   patients.

7. Fill in the placeholders that only you can decide.
   - Terms & Conditions -> "Governing Law" section currently says the
     jurisdiction should be finalized once there's a formal publisher of
     record - put your actual business/jurisdiction there.
   - Privacy Policy / Terms / FAQ -> "contact the developer at the support
     address provided with your copy of the App" - decide on and add a
     real support email address in these documents.

8. Decide on and communicate pricing/licensing (referenced in the FAQ but
   intentionally left for you to define outside the app).

4) DEEP-DIVE: MOBILE DENTAL-CLINIC APPS YOU ASKED ABOUT
---------------------------------------------------------
You asked specifically about "Dental Cases," "My Dental Clinic," "My Dental
Clinic Online," and similar apps. Unlike the earlier section (which named
big desktop suites like Dentrix/Curve from general knowledge), this section
is from actually pulling app-store listings, vendor pages, and reviews for
each app below.

First, a correction: "Dental Cases" (the literal app by that name on the
App Store) turned out to be an exam-prep/education app for dental students
studying for the CDA exam - case-based learning modules, a dental
dictionary, note-taking for studying. It is not a practice-management or
charting tool and isn't a real competitor to Stom. If you meant a
different app by that name, let me know and I'll look again.

--- My Dental Clinic / My Dental Clinic Online (QX Information Technology
    Corp / "Quantum X") ---
Two related apps from the same small vendor (Philippines-based). "My
Dental Clinic" is the older/legacy app; "My Dental Clinic Online" is the
newer cloud-synced version with a public patient-booking portal.

What they have:
- A visual per-tooth dental chart ("dental charting") - real, but the
  vendor's own marketing never goes beyond the word "chart." No mention of
  per-surface marking, standardized status codes (decay/filling/crown/
  missing/implant), or which tooth-numbering system is used. Likely
  simpler than what Stom already has.
- Free-text notes attached to the chart, plus generic "attach dental
  images" (no DICOM mentioned anywhere - images are almost certainly just
  JPG/PNG photo attachments, not real radiographs).
- Appointment list/calendar, SMS reminders (paid tiers), recall reminders,
  and (Online only) a public-facing booking portal patients can request
  appointments through, plus a one-tap "request a Zoom meeting" button.
- Cash payment recording, installment tracking, expense tracking, basic
  and "advanced" financial reports, PDF/Excel export and import.
- Multi-dentist support (up to 4 dentists + 1 secretary) with role-based
  permissions - web-version only, paid tier.
- Tiered cloud backup (2.5-10GB depending on plan), offline-first with
  sync, explicit "you can download your whole database anytime" data
  portability promise.
- PIN code app lock (paid tiers), self-declared HIPAA compliance and
  "SSL AES 256" encryption (marketing language, no third-party audit/
  certification referenced anywhere).
- Multi-language UI (Spanish, Russian, Arabic, Portuguese) even on the
  free tier.
- A separate always-free "Screening" mini-product: a shareable public link
  clinics send patients to fill out a pre-visit intake/screening form.
- Fragmented, somewhat inconsistent pricing across ~6-7 SKUs (free up to
  ~$30/mo); a real user review (iOS, 2024) reported a treatment-plan bug
  where procedures got duplicated or lost.

--- Dentulu / Dentulu Pro ("DentuluPro") ---
A much bigger, telemedicine-first platform - patient-facing (Dentulu) and
dentist-facing (DentuluPro) apps, not just a records tool.

What they have:
- "Electronic patient charts" and "perio charting" are named features, but
  after checking every feature page and both app-store listings, there is
  no evidence of an actual visual tooth-by-tooth odontogram - the
  charting language is generic ("clinical documentation," "treatment
  notes") or specifically periodontal (pocket depth/bleeding/recession),
  not a full-mouth tooth chart. This is a real gap in a platform that
  otherwise looks very polished.
- DICOM is explicitly accepted as an upload format, but only in one
  specific "second opinion" flow - not confirmed as a general viewer, and
  there's no evidence they actually render/window DICOM images the way
  Stom's DICOM importer now does (parse + display with proper contrast).
- Live video consultations, async photo consults, 24/7 emergency
  on-demand dentists, and paid second-opinion reviews ($149 dentist
  review, $20 AI X-ray review) - teledentistry is their core differentiator.
- AI radiograph analysis for caries/bone loss/calculus - but this turned
  out to be a licensed third-party integration (Pearl AI, FDA-cleared),
  not Dentulu's own in-house model. A separate, vaguer "60 oral health
  parameters" AI claim for general photos has no published breakdown of
  what it actually checks.
- A bundled home intraoral camera ("MouthCAM," $99, free with annual
  membership) - notably the wired version is NOT iOS-compatible, a real
  gap in their own hardware line.
- Electronic consent/medical-clearance forms, e-prescriptions, 2-way
  encrypted messaging, a no-login time-limited file-sharing feature
  ("Dental Drop") for sending images/records to outside labs/specialists.
- Multi-provider network model - hygienists and specialists can join and
  earn through the platform, not just clinic staff accounts.
- Pricing is genuinely inconsistent across their own pages ($79-99/mo
  provider plans, conflicting setup fees between pages) - and reviews
  flagged real problems: paid AI X-ray reviews failing to load, broken
  chat, unanswered "free consultation" calls redirected to a $55 paid
  call, and providers who signed up and never got a single referral.

--- Smaller/other mobile dental apps checked ---
- DentiCalc: not a practice-management tool at all - a chairside
  patient-education and treatment-cost-quoting tool (photo/video library +
  pricing calculator). No chart, no records, no scheduling.
- AppointDent: small, new (2026), scheduling + basic patient notes app.
  No tooth chart, no image upload, no billing.
- Dentem: the most full-featured of the small apps - scheduling, patient
  profiles, medical history, treatment plans, image upload (X-rays/photos,
  not DICOM), SMS/email reminders, staff management. No confirmed tooth
  chart.
- Clinicia: not dental-specific (a general multi-specialty clinic system
  with a "dental" skin) - but has real strengths Stom and the others
  don't: queue/waiting-room management, inventory/stock alerts, and
  actual dental lab order management (crown/bridge/implant specs, shade,
  trial dates). No tooth chart, no DICOM.
- iDentalSoft: no real native app - "mobile" just means their web app
  works in a mobile browser.
- Open Dental: desktop-only core, but a real mobile ecosystem around it -
  their own ODMobile (schedule/texting/e-prescribing/view-only images) and
  eClipboard (paperless intake) apps, plus third-party companion apps like
  FlexMobile built on Open Dental's API. None of the mobile pieces do
  clinical charting - that stays desktop-only.

Across every single app researched here, none confirmed real DICOM
support with actual image rendering/windowing - at best, DICOM is
accepted as an upload format without evidence of proper decoding, most
never mention it at all. That's a genuine, verified point of difference
for Stom, not just a guess.

--- What this means for Stom ---

Where Stom is already ahead of everything researched:
- Real DICOM decoding (uncompressed, JPEG Baseline, JPEG Lossless) with
  proper windowing/contrast - nobody else researched actually does this.
- A real, working visual tooth-by-tooth odontogram - several competitors
  either don't have one (Dentulu, Clinicia) or it's unclear how deep
  theirs goes (My Dental Clinic, Dentem).

Real gaps worth considering, roughly ordered by how common they are
across competitors (i.e. how much they're apparently expected) vs. how
much work they'd take:

1. Appointment scheduling/calendar - every single competitor researched
   has this in some form. Biggest, most consistent gap.
2. SMS/communication (reminders at minimum, 2-way texting in the fuller
   apps) - very common, pairs naturally with scheduling.
3. PIN/app-lock security - cheap to add, present even in the free/cheap
   tier of the simplest competitor (My Dental Clinic).
4. Multi-user support (secretary/front-desk account, multiple dentists
   sharing one clinic) - common in every paid tier across competitors;
   Stom is currently single-device/no-accounts by design.
5. Billing/expense tracking - common (My Dental Clinic, Clinicia, Dentem);
   even a basic "cost per treatment + running total" would close this gap
   without building a full accounting module.
6. Treatment plans as a distinct structured thing (procedure + status +
   cost), not just free-text notes - matches the "quick-pick procedure
   list" idea from the earlier feature-ideas section, and is explicitly
   named by both My Dental Clinic and Dentulu.
7. Data export (PDF/Excel per patient) - simple, present in My Dental
   Clinic's paid tier, useful for referrals/insurance.
8. Patient-facing booking/self-scheduling portal - present in My Dental
   Clinic Online and Dentulu; bigger scope, only worth it once internal
   scheduling exists.
9. Telemedicine/video consults and AI radiograph analysis - Dentulu's
   whole pitch, but a much larger investment (and their execution has
   real reported problems - broken AI reviews, unanswered calls). Given
   Stom's current positioning as a focused charting tool done well, this
   is worth treating as a "maybe eventually," not a near-term priority.

Suggested sequencing, factoring in this new research alongside the
earlier feature list: appointment scheduling first (the one gap every
competitor has and Stom doesn't), then PIN lock (cheap, real security
value), then treatment-plan structure + billing basics together (they
naturally share data), then multi-user accounts once there's something
worth sharing access to.
